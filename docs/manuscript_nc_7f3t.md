
# Distribution-free error control for cell type annotation in spatial proteomics

---

## Abstract

Cell type annotation limits the throughput of spatial proteomics, yet classifiers report confidence rather than the error rate among the labels a laboratory would keep. We introduce a post hoc, distribution-free layer that turns any cell type classifier into a commit-or-review rule with a finite-sample bound on selective error. Across 39,684 expert-labelled cells, a 20% error budget committed 86.4% of cells without review at a realised selective error of 0.14. Coverage was 1.00 under exchangeable resampling, 0.82 across fields of view and 0.40 under cross-tissue transfer. The failure was visible as excess acceptance relative to an oracle, so the layer reports when its own guarantee no longer applies. Target calibration restored coverage, and a real antibody-panel change held coverage at 1.00 with acceptance 0.50 at a 10% budget. We package the result as a decision procedure, a label-free audit and a price list in target labels.

**Keywords:** Spatial Proteomics, Cell Type Annotation, Conformal Prediction, Selective Error Control, Distribution-Free Inference

---

## Introduction

Multiplexed imaging now measures dozens of proteins per cell in situ, and the resulting cell tables drive much of spatial proteomics [1,2]. Each segmented cell needs a type, and experts still assign most labels. This annotation step limits throughput. CellSighter and CellTune have made automated typing practical [3,4], but neither reports how many of the labels it retains are wrong.

A softmax score is not an error rate. It is uncalibrated on the training distribution and changes under distribution shift [5]. Spatial proteomics is exposed to such shift because the field has no standard marker panel [6]. Studies choose different antibodies, protocols and tissues, so a model trained on one cohort is often applied to another with partial channel overlap [7,8]. A fixed confidence threshold therefore does not carry the same meaning across cohorts [9].

A laboratory needs a review budget, not another score. It needs to know how many cells to inspect so that the error rate among retained labels stays below a stated level. Conformal prediction and conformal risk control provide finite-sample bounds with few distributional assumptions [10,11]. Applying them to spatial proteomics is not the advance. The obstacle is exchangeability, which cross-tissue and cross-panel deployment violates. A usable procedure must either survive that violation or price it.

We build a post hoc layer around the classifier. It controls the two unconditional probabilities that define selective error; the natural conditional alternative fails when the accepted set and the calibration block come apart [12,13]. The layer is valid when calibration represents deployment and fails visibly when it does not. We turn that boundary into a label-free audit and a price list in target labels. We test the layer on a cross-tissue and cross-panel benchmark, on independent cohorts and on two published classifiers.

Three operational questions follow. First, can a threshold be certified without assuming a particular classifier or a parametric error model? Second, what does the certificate mean when calibration and deployment differ, as they do across images, tissues and antibody panels? Third, when the certificate fails, can a laboratory choose between reviewing more cells and buying more target labels? We address these questions with 39,684 expert-labelled cells, a split design that separates exchangeable from non-exchangeable deployment, and a label-free check of marker consistency. We report positive and negative replications, including two published classifiers and two real panel changes. The intended output is not another classifier but a decision procedure that states what can be committed, what must be reviewed and what a repair costs. This framing also makes failure informative: an empty accepted set, an exhausted budget or an over-budget ratio becomes a usable signal rather than a silent degradation of confidence.

## Results

We first test whether confidence reports error under transfer. We then define the controller, locate its boundary, price the guarantee and its repair, audit marker consistency, and test whether the behaviour replicates outside the source depot.

### A benchmark where confidence is not an error rate

We assembled a benchmark from CellTuneDepot, a public resource of multiplexed imaging cohorts with expert annotation [14]. Four cohorts met our inclusion criteria: breast, colorectal, graft versus host disease (GVHD) and melanoma. They contain 41,602 expert-labelled cells, of which 39,684 have a defined type (Fig. 1A). The cohorts span three tissues and four panels; pairwise Jaccard overlap between panels is 0.18 to 0.44 (Fig. 1B). The transfer family uses only shared channels, so its twelve pairs, with 11 to 27 channels each, form a re-panelling stress test rather than an antibody replacement. The real replacement test appears below.

Three audit findings shaped the design. The GVHD cohort has a delivery defect. Its annotation table and shipped segmentation mask share identifiers for 17.4% of cells, 1,794 of 10,283, and the two files use disjoint ranges at field-of-view level (Fig. 1D) [15]. Colorectal and GVHD also ship semi-supervised labels of unknown accuracy; these disagree with expert annotation in 27% and 15% of cells (Fig. 1C). Finally, 0 to 4 of the 12 to 30 Boolean rules per cohort could not be evaluated on the available channels. We therefore treat the expert labels as the reference and keep the delivery audit separate from the error analysis.

If confidence were an error probability, a fixed threshold would mean the same thing in every cohort. Under a random split it does: expected calibration error is 0.08 on both sides. Under cross-tissue transfer it rises from 0.07 to 0.20, a factor of 2.8, while accuracy falls from 0.77 to 0.58 (Fig. 2E,F). On the melanoma target the model is correct about 30% of the time at a confidence of 0.5. Cross-field-of-view splitting leaves calibration error unchanged. Cross-tissue transfer multiplies it by 2.4 and costs 19 points of accuracy (Fig. 2H). The degradation belongs to the change of domain, not to the softmax.

### A distribution-free controller with a visible boundary

We wrap the classifier in a post hoc layer. Let s(x) be one minus the maximum softmax probability, and let err(x) indicate that the prediction is wrong. For a threshold lambda the layer accepts cells with s(x) at most lambda and controls the selective error R(lambda) = E[err(x) 1{s(x) <= lambda}] / E[1{s(x) <= lambda}]. Given a budget alpha and a confidence level 1 - delta, it returns lambda_hat = max {lambda in grid : N+(lambda) <= alpha D-(lambda)}. Here N+ and D- are high-probability upper and lower limits on the two expectations. The guarantee is P(R(lambda_hat) <= alpha) >= 1 - delta. It is finite sample and assumes only that the calibration sample is exchangeable with deployment. An empty accepted set counts as a vacuous success.

The construction is the technical content, and the obvious route fails. A conditional binomial bound treats the number of wrong accepted cells as a binomial draw from the accepted count. It is valid only when the error probability is constant among accepted cells, which no score guarantees. A score that ranks by confidence accepts a mixture with different error probabilities, and a binomial with the mixture mean does not stochastically dominate that mixture. We therefore place exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson limits on the two unconditional probabilities and recover their ratio algebraically. A Bonferroni share delta/(2m) over the m candidate thresholds carries the uniformity over the grid. Under shift or an atomic score this conditional route accepts nearly three times as many cells and covers 0.61 and 0.37 against a nominal 0.90. Error heterogeneity visible to the score leaves it at 0.97. The unconditional bound costs about 2.5 points of acceptance.

We evaluated three split families. In the random split the held-out pool is divided into exchangeable calibration and evaluation halves. In the cross-field-of-view split the halves come from disjoint images of one tissue and panel. In the cross-tissue split the model is trained and calibrated on one cohort and evaluated on another, which breaks exchangeability by design and is the deployment regime of interest.

Under the random split coverage is 1.00 over 19 units and 570 evaluations, with 21.2% vacuous evaluations. It is 0.996 at a 5% budget and 1.00 at 20%. A Monte Carlo study with 4,000 repetitions returns 1.00 at 200 and 500 calibration cells and 0.997 at 2,000. It stays at 1.00 when the error rate varies in a way the score cannot see (Fig. 3C). Cross-field-of-view coverage is 0.83, 0.82 and 0.92 at 5, 10 and 20%, and 0.75, 0.76 and 0.90 after vacuous decisions are removed. Cross-tissue coverage is 0.54, 0.40 and 0.29. At a 10% budget 38.9% of transfer evaluations returned an empty accepted set and were counted as covered. Among committed decisions coverage is 0.01, 0.01 and 0.06 (Table 1).

The failure is visible in the controller output. The oracle is the best threshold available, so a controller that holds its budget cannot systematically exceed it. The ratio of certified to oracle acceptance is 0.74 under the random split, 0.77 across fields of view and 3.05 under cross-tissue transfer. It ranges from 1.56 to 30.7 across cohorts (Fig. 4C). Over 1,690 evaluation units at a 10% budget the ratio exceeds one in 575, and every one is over budget. It never exceeds one among the 1,115 units within budget. On an independent cohort the rule misses 60 of 174 over-budget transfer evaluations, all cases where the oracle accepts nothing. It is a screening rule, not a theorem.

### The review budget and the price of a repair

Pooling the 19,669 held-out cells of the random split family, a 10% budget certifies 11,742 cells and routes 7,927 to review. It commits 59.7% of cells without review at a realised selective error of 0.06. A 20% budget commits 86.4% at 0.14 (Fig. 5A). The 10% budget defers 40.3% of cells against 24.4% for the oracle, about 16 extra points of human review (Fig. 5A). The 13.6% that still reaches a human at a 20% budget reflects the ranking quality of the underlying classifier and the assay; it is not evidence that the guarantee failed. This is a labour allocation result, not a validity claim.

A valid certificate must be compared with the oracle at equal realised selective error, and it does not recover oracle throughput. At matched selective error the certificate recovers 0.57 of oracle acceptance at a 5% budget and 0.91 at 30%. The shortfall is not sampling noise, with a 95% interval of 0.62 to 0.83 on the ratio, and no valid method can exceed the oracle. The uncalibrated threshold accepts 0.75 of the batch against 0.56 for the certificate. It holds the budget in 58.2% of evaluations against 100.0%, in 0.57 against 0.82 across fields of view, and in 0.16 against 0.40 under transfer. The apparent 3.05 under transfer is a broken guarantee, not better control.

Target calibration restores coverage by construction. Coverage among committed decisions is 0.998 at a 10% budget against 0.04 for the source-calibrated controller. Seven of the twelve cross-tissue pairs become valid and non-vacuous within 2,000 target labels. The five that never do are limited by the transferred ranking, not by label count; three have an oracle that accepts under 2% of cells. Within a cohort, 500 uniformly drawn target labels leave acceptance at 0.53 while lifting coverage from 0.82 to 1.00. Spending half that budget on a refit accepts 0.36 of the batch at 2,000 labels, against 0.04 for the frozen model. Table 2 counts vacuous evaluations as covered, so the committed readout is the one to plan with.

Run end to end, the layer removed 134.0 of 159.1 expert hours at a 5% budget and 147.7 h at 20% on a public liver cohort. The cohort has 28,646 cells over nine channels, four donor sets held out in turn, and 500 labels of each new set spent on calibration. Realised selective errors were 0.017 and 0.046. A threshold tuned on the same 500 labels held the 5% budget in half the donor sets, against every one for the certificate. The guarantee buys validity, not the smallest possible review queue.

### A label-free audit of labels and markers

Reference labels and classifiers can be interrogated without held-out data. We compiled each cohort's published Boolean definitions into an executable rule set and measured the fraction of cells that violate the definition of their assigned type. A single pooled rate misleads. A first pass returned 5.66% for melanoma and 4.02% for GVHD, mostly from rules that reference missing channels or from an ambiguous separator in the depot syntax [16]. Reading the colorectal definitions as a conjunction rather than a disjunction moves their rate from 0.58% to 18.1%. Across 68 conventions per cohort the pooled rate spans two orders of magnitude, so the sweep identifies the verdict rather than the level. Five cell types clear a 5% gate under every convention, and colorectal APC stays below it under every convention; the full convention sweep is reported in Supplementary Table 14 and Supplementary Figs. 7 and 9. Accepted cells are more marker-consistent than deferred ones in 1,590 of 1,694 estimable units, with a paired sign test P < 0.001 and a median difference of 0.12. This audit is independent of held-out labels. It measures consistency with published marker logic, not correctness against an external gold standard.

### External replication and published classifiers

The benchmark shares one depot and one pipeline. We next break both. The STELLAR CODEX intestine cohort has 248,285 cells over 48 markers, and the combined tonsil and Barrett cohort has 219,926 cells over 44 markers [17,18]. Each was subsampled to 20,000 cells. Intestine reproduces exchangeable coverage 1.00 with acceptance 0.85 at a 10% budget. Image-grouped splitting accepts 0.73 at selective error 0.08 but covers 0.66 (Fig. 6A). Transfer to tonsil and Barrett on 26 shared markers covers 0.03 (Fig. 6B). A multiplexed ion beam microarray, with 345,490 cells over 15 markers, and a liver imaging mass cytometry cohort, with 109,245 cells over 11 channels, cover 0.993 and 1.00 at 10% [19,20]. IMMUcan panel 1 V3 has 57,791 labels over 37 markers from 30 samples. Under a protocol locked before the endpoint, coverage is 1.00 at 5, 10 and 20% over five sample-grouped folds, ten draws and three classifiers, with acceptance 0.90, 0.99 and 1.00 (Table 3). The disjoint V1 batch has 290,226 labels from 149 samples and reproduces coverage 1.00 with acceptance 0.935, 0.999 and 1.000.

The strongest cross-panel test replaced the antibody panel rather than dropping channels. We trained on STELLAR small intestine, 235,874 labelled cells over 48 markers, and deployed to an independent adult duodenum CODEX atlas, 211,424 cells over 42 channels from three donors and 107 ROIs [21]. Fourteen markers were shared, and their matrix rows were resolved before any error endpoint was computed. Target labels entered calibration only. At a 10% budget, an equal-weight ensemble covered 1.00 [0.93, 1.00] with realised selective error 0.09 and acceptance 0.50. Source-only calibration covered 0.00 with selective error 0.25. At 20%, acceptance rose to 0.83 with coverage 1.00 and selective error 0.19. The same 14 markers supported acceptance 0.83 under in-domain training, so the shortfall belongs to the cross-laboratory and cross-panel shift. Calibrating on other ROIs gave coverage 0.88 [0.76, 0.94] at 10% and 0.90 [0.79, 0.96] at 20%, locating ROI batch effects as the remaining boundary. A per-marker rank transform raised acceptance from 0.21 under a per-domain z-score to 0.50 for the ensemble. The cost of a real panel change is therefore paid in labels and throughput, not in validity.

A second real panel change, across 27 shared channels in the microarray pair, was harder. Calibrating on 100 to 8,000 target labels held coverage at 1.00. Acceptance reached only 0.03 at a 10% budget and 0.20 at 20%, against 0.40 and 0.70 for the source-calibrated arm. A refit repaired the change at 10%. On 1,000 target labels, net gain was +0.03 on a 5,458-cell colon core, +0.55 on a 23,081-cell breast cohort and +0.04 on a 65,498-cell tonsil cohort. On the tonsil pair, 4,000 labels returned +0.44. A second colon source marks the boundary. At 8,153 cells, 1,000 labels leave the certificate silent and 4,000 labels are half the batch, so no rung is net positive (minus 0.28). The label-to-batch ratio, not the certificate, sets that boundary.

A 250-label pilot can price the repair. A threshold chosen on four folds and applied to the fifth admitted 43 of 45 same-tissue and 2 of 36 cross-platform units, retained 0.371 against 0.392 for always repairing and cut the cross-platform loss from 0.095 to 0.031. The pilot is a gate, not a universal step; its verdict depended on the base model, and prospective validation remains future work.

We also wrapped two published tools built differently. CellSighter is a convolutional network over image crops, vendored unmodified. CellTune is reimplemented from its documentation as its default XGBoost and CatBoost pair because it ships as a desktop application. Both certify on the exchangeable split at a 10% budget. CellSighter retains 0.81 of cells at selective error 0.07 against 0.90 for an oracle, and CellTune retains 0.96 at selective error 0.06. Both fail under transfer with the same signature: every evaluation is over budget, selective error exceeds 0.30, ratios are 10.6 and 39.9, and accuracy falls to 0.38 and 0.43. A third pair, GVHD to colorectal, repeats the pattern with 0.88 retained at selective error 0.07 and a transfer ratio of 5.5.

### From a certificate to a deployment decision

The procedure is packaged as one run that leaves the classifier unchanged (Fig. 7A). On the liver cohort, 28,646 cells from four donor-held-out units were scored with a fixed classifier, and 500 target labels were spent on calibration (Fig. 7B). At a 5% budget the layer committed 91.2% of cells at a realised selective error of 0.017, left 2,523 cells for review and saved 134.0 of 159.1 expert hours. At 20% it committed 99.8% at 0.046, left 65 cells and saved 147.7 hours. These totals deduct the calibration labels, so they measure net review removed rather than gross acceptance. A 250-label pilot then acts as a deployment gate (Fig. 7C). Leave-one-fold-out decisions admitted 43 of 45 same-tissue units and 2 of 36 cross-platform units. The gate raised the cross-platform mean net share by 0.064 [0.032, 0.092] and cost the same-tissue arm 0.022 [-0.054, 0.000]. The pilot separates deployments likely to repay a repair from those where the label price exceeds the review saved. It does not establish validity; the certificate does that.

## Discussion

The contribution is a decision layer, not a better classifier. It tells a laboratory which labels from any cell type classifier can be kept at a stated error budget, with finite-sample validity. It changes the question from which model is best to which labels can be kept. The layer is valid when calibration represents deployment and visibly broken when it does not. That boundary is part of the result, not a caveat.

The closest method is conformal risk control. Our increment is a construction for the two unconditional probabilities that define selective error, together with a negative result. The natural conditional binomial bound is invalid once the accepted set and the calibration block diverge, and it fails under shift or an atomic score. Unconditional bounds cost about 2.5 points of acceptance. Cross-field-of-view splitting leaves coverage 0.82 at a 10% budget, 8 points below nominal. Cells from different images of one tissue were therefore not exchangeable enough to preserve the nominal guarantee [22]. Cross-tissue transfer fails more sharply, with coverage 0.40 at 10% and 0.01 among committed decisions [23]. Weighted conformal control restores coverage without exchangeability, but Proposition 3 prices it. A weighted Bernstein certificate needs O(M log(1/delta)/(alpha beta)) labels and a rare-stratum construction forces Omega(M log(1/delta)/alpha). For beta bounded below the price is Theta(M/alpha), so the weighted arm accepts nothing because its label price binds [24,25].

The practical answer is a decision rule priced in target labels. Across fields of view, 500 target labels restored coverage without reducing acceptance. Seven of the twelve cross-tissue pairs became valid and non-vacuous within 2,000 labels. A real panel change is repaired by spending labels on the model, not only on the calibrator. The label-to-batch ratio sets the boundary. A 250-label pilot can act as a gate for deployments whose repairs often fail, but it is not a universal step [26]. At deployment, a certified-to-oracle ratio above one indicates that calibration does not represent the batch.

External validation includes a positive, non-simulated panel change. Target calibration on 14 shared markers covered 1.00 at a 10% budget, while source-only calibration covered 0.00. Acceptance was 0.50 with a rank-normalised ensemble and 0.21 under the per-domain z-score, and ROI-disjoint calibration exposed the batch boundary. Earlier cohorts reproduce the guarantee when the split is exchangeable and its failure when it is not [19,20]. A second IMMUcan annotation batch, disjoint from the first, adds 290,226 labels and reproduces coverage 1.00 with acceptance 0.935 at a 5% budget. Accepted cells were more consistent with published marker logic than deferred ones, a positive signal that does not use held-out labels. The limits are clear. The gold standard covers three tissues and four panels. A systematic annotation bias shared across cohorts would be invisible. Marker definitions are contested, and the audit returns a population verdict rather than a level. No cohort pair is prospective. The 13.6% residual review at a 20% budget reflects the score and the assay rather than a failure of validity.

Deployment should begin with a small calibration pilot drawn from the target batch. The pilot has two jobs. It supplies labels for the certificate, and it gives an early estimate of whether a repair is likely to repay its cost. If the pilot admits repair, labels should go first to calibration and then to a refit, because calibration restores validity whereas refitting changes throughput. If the pilot rejects repair, the defensible choice is to review the batch or change the classifier, not to lower the nominal budget. Reporting should also keep three quantities separate: the nominal budget, the realised selective error among committed labels and the share of the batch left for review. Conflating them invites the mistaken conclusion that a low review fraction is evidence of validity. The residual review at a fixed budget is a property of the score and of the assay, and it should be planned as a labour cost rather than presented as a failure of the guarantee.

The framework should transfer beyond this benchmark. A reference atlas and an annotation method are the interface it attaches to in single-cell transcriptomics [27,28,29]. Spatial transcriptomics admits the same construction with marker gene logic in place of protein logic. Imaging mass cytometry, CODEX and multiplexed ion beam imaging differ in channel count and noise but pose the same statistical problem [30,31,32]. The next step is to embed the price list in an active learning loop, so that new labels are spent where they buy the most certified throughput.

## Methods

### Data

The primary benchmark used CellTuneDepot, which provides manual annotations with a per-field-of-view segmentation mask and semi-supervised labels as per-cell mean intensities, areas and centroids for colorectal and GVHD. The four cohorts contain 7,610, 2,505, 10,283 and 21,204 expert labels. After excluding coarse unidentified cells, 7,450, 2,397, 9,504 and 20,333 remain usable. The panels have 22, 58, 40 and 40 markers, and the cohorts have 13, 12, 30 and 19 Boolean rules. In total, 41,602 expert-labelled and 39,684 usable cells enter the analysis.

External cohorts were the public STELLAR CODEX intestine and tonsil data, the MIBI tissue microarray and the liver imaging mass cytometry cohort. We also used the adult duodenum CODEX atlas and IMMUcan panel 1 V3 and V1. IMMUcan V3 and V1 contributed 57,791 and 290,226 labels over 37 markers from 30 and 149 disjoint samples, split by sample. The duodenum atlas contributed 211,424 labelled cells over 42 channels from three donors and 107 ROIs. The cross-panel replication used the 14 markers CD31, CD4, CD8, CD44, CD3, CD90, aSMA, CD206, CD138, CD19, CD163, HLA-DR, PDPN and VIM. Nineteen markers are shared by name. Five further shared markers, CD45, CD11c, CD69, Ki67 and CD15, could not be assigned a matrix row with confidence from the published annotations. They were excluded on row-identity grounds before any error endpoint was computed. No marker was added or removed on the basis of classification performance. Target labels entered calibration only.

We audited delivery integrity by intersecting each labelled field of view's shipped segmentation identifiers with those in the annotation table. No result depends on the delivery defect because the features of the two affected cohorts come from the per-cell table, where labels join at full coverage.

### Preprocessing and marker logic

One table per cohort is built along two paths. Colorectal and GVHD take per-cell mean intensities, areas and centroids from the shipped table. Breast and melanoma decode each field's channel images and segmentation mask. For each mask label, this gives each channel's mean over the cell pixels, the pixel count and the mask centroid. Preprocessing applies a log transform and then z-scores each marker. The transform is fitted once on the training cells and applied to training, calibration and deployment. The transfer family is the exception: it fits two domains and therefore uses unlabelled deployment features. Each cohort defines its populations by a Boolean expression over channels. A recursive descent parser evaluates the expression and canonicalises every identifier, so a rule written for one panel can be evaluated on another. Four colorectal definitions use a semicolon where the others use a conjunction or an alternative. The alternative reading is the default. A marker is called positive at the mixture crossing point of the cohort's own populations.

### Selective error control

The controller is calibrated on a labelled sample exchangeable with the deployment population. It bounds the two expectations that define selective error rather than their ratio. The numerator takes an exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper limit at confidence 1 - delta/(2m). The denominator takes an exact lower limit at the same level, where m is the size of the candidate threshold grid. The ratio of the two limits is compared with the budget. Without that multiplicity share the same threshold covers the budget in only 0.58 of evaluations. The alternatives compared are a conditional binomial route, an empirical Bernstein variant, a density-ratio weighted variant, a Mondrian variant per predicted class, and split conformal prediction sets. Defaults are delta 0.10, a minimum acceptance of 25 cells, a grid of 120 thresholds and the score one minus the maximum predicted probability.

### Split families and the label-cost ladder

In the exchangeable family, the fields of view of one cohort are shuffled into five folds. One fold is held out for training, and its cells are split at random into calibration and evaluation halves in ten independent draws. In the within-cohort family, the deployment set is one fold, the calibration pool is the next and the remaining three train the model. In the transfer family, the model is trained and calibrated on a source cohort and deployed on another. The analysis is restricted to the canonical panel intersection of 11 to 27 channels over the twelve ordered pairs. Classes must have at least 30 source and 20 target cells.

The label-cost ladder draws k cells at random from the deployment pool. It calibrates on those k cells alone and evaluates on the rest, so the two are exchangeable at any k. A model adaptation arm splits the k cells in half. It refits on the source training set plus one half and calibrates the refitted model on the other. In the duodenum replication the five target ROI folds were each split ten times into exchangeable calibration and evaluation halves. Three classifiers were trained on STELLAR, and their equal-weight ensemble was evaluated. Marker features were normalised in two label-free ways. The z-score pipeline applies log1p, subtracts the domain mean and divides by the domain standard deviation. The rank pipeline applies log1p, maps each marker to its within-domain empirical quantile and then to a standard normal score. Both transforms were fitted separately inside each domain and used no labels. The rank pipeline is reported as the operating point because the z-score left the linear model with an uninformative confidence ranking. The ROI-disjoint arm calibrated on 10,000 labels from the other four folds and evaluated on the held-out fold.

### Evaluation metrics

Selective error is the fraction of accepted cells whose predicted class differs from the reference label, and it is undefined when nothing is accepted. Acceptance is the accepted fraction of deployment cells. Coverage is the fraction of evaluation units whose realised selective error is at most the budget. An empty accepted set counts as covered, and the fraction of empty decisions is stored separately. The fraction of oracle acceptance divides the controller's acceptance by that of a threshold chosen with the deployment labels in hand. Expected calibration error bins the confidence into at most 15 quantile bins and returns the bin-count weighted mean absolute difference between mean confidence and empirical accuracy.

### Classifiers, compute and reproducibility

The certificate propositions, including the bounded-ratio price, are in `docs/propositions.md`. Three off-the-shelf classifiers were left untuned because the contribution is the layer above them: multinomial logistic regression, a random forest and histogram gradient boosting. A comparison arm bagged the two cheaper learners five times to form maximum probability, entropy and mutual information scores [33,34,35]. All code is Python 3.10 with numpy, pandas, scikit-learn, scipy and matplotlib and runs on CPU. The CellSighter wrap imports PyTorch and also runs there. A single command re-derives the four negative results and the four positive replications from the released tables and exits non-zero on mismatch. A preflight check re-derives every number quoted here.

### The layer as a tool

The procedure leaves the classifier untouched and is illustrated in Fig. 7A. A laboratory annotates a few hundred to two thousand random target cells, scores the batch with any probabilistic classifier, fixes the error budget and commits cells under the certified threshold. The remainder is reviewed in order of uncertainty. Arriving labels then update the audit: a certified-to-oracle acceptance ratio above one indicates that calibration does not represent the batch, and the price list states whether more labels or a refit is the cheaper repair. The same command certifies a labelled table, deploys on a new batch, repairs a shifted batch, or accepts a batch whose labels exist only for a calibration pilot. It writes per-cell decisions, a report and the budget figure.
